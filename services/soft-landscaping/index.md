## Creating garden beds that work with Canberra's climate.

New garden beds and borders don't just happen. It takes plant selection that matches your climate and aspect, good soil prep, proper planting technique, and mulching to lock in moisture and keep weeds down. That's where we come in.

Kai helps with the whole process. Canberra's frost-hardy natives like grevillea, banksia, correa, and westringia thrive here and support local wildlife. If you want ornamentals — lavender, rosemary, camellia, Japanese maple — we can mix those in for formal character and year-round interest. The key is choosing plants that match your soil and sunlight so they actually grow instead of just surviving.

We prepare garden beds properly: dig out existing grass or debris, amend the soil with organic matter to improve drainage and structure, edge the beds cleanly, and mulch everything to retain moisture and suppress weeds. Water-wise plants that reduce irrigation needs once established are our default — good for your water bill and the environment.

This is soft landscaping — the planting side. If you need hard landscaping like retaining walls, paving, or decking, Kai can refer you to a specialist. But for creating and planting new beds, borders, and green spaces that actually thrive in Canberra's climate, we've got it covered.

## Common questions about soft landscaping and new garden beds.

### What plants grow well in Canberra's climate?

Canberra's climate suits a wide range of plants. Frost-hardy natives like grevillea, banksia, correa, and westringia thrive and support local wildlife. Ornamentals like lavender, rosemary, camellia, and Japanese maple add variety and formal character. The key is choosing plants that match your site's soil and sunlight — once established, most of these reduce irrigation needs significantly.

### Can you design and plant a native garden?

Absolutely. We can design native gardens from scratch, or mix natives with ornamentals to suit your preference. Native gardens reduce irrigation needs, support local wildlife, and look intentional when the plant selection matches your soil and aspect. Kai can advise on plant selection, spacing, soil prep, and mulching to give your native garden the best start.

### What's the difference between soft landscaping and hard landscaping?

Soft landscaping is plants, soil, mulch, and green features. Hard landscaping is non-living structures like retaining walls, paving, decking, edging, and pergolas. We do the soft side — garden beds, plant selection, planting, soil improvement, and mulching. For hard landscaping, Kai can refer you to a specialist contractor.

### When is the best time to plant new garden beds in Canberra?

Spring (Sept–Nov) and autumn (Feb–May) are the sweet spots — soil is warm enough for good root growth, and there's usually enough rainfall to support new plants. Summer planting is possible but requires more watering. Winter is not ideal unless the soil stays relatively warm. Kai can advise based on your specific plants and site conditions.
